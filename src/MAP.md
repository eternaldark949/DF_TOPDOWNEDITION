# Source map

Every piece of *Dimensions: Freelancer (top-down)* and what lives in it. `tools/build.mjs` joins them, **in the order below**, into the playable `DFAB V8.html`.
Order matters: a file can only use, when it loads, what the files above it declared.

For editor/runtime batches that change static building or foliage geometry, reach, or same-length collections, call `game.invalidateRenderSpatial()` after the batch. It also invalidates effect and physics indexes. Physics-only edits can use `game.invalidatePhysicsSpatial(items)`; lamp/puddle edits within the same tick/draw can use `game.invalidateEffectSpatial()`. Map loading and known wall removal call the relevant hooks automatically.

## world/
| File | What's in it |
|---|---|
| `world/navigation.js` | `NavGrid` pathfinding, companion tactics and steering Geometry is re-read once per simulation tick (`_refresh`). Each revision records where it changed (`_changes`), so a cached path re-plans only when a change touches its box (`pathTouched`); a goal that moves ≤32 px re-aims the path's last leg when that's clear; a failed search waits `NAV.FAIL_RETRY_TICKS`. A* runs the exact swept test only between cells marked `_near` geometry (within radius + half a cell diagonal), which gives identical paths. `actorSteer` returns `remaining`, and with no route it edges straight on where a step is clear. |
| `world/walker.js` | **`Walker.goTo(actor, x, y, { face, speed, arrive, timeout, via, onArrive, onFail })`**: walks any procedural humanoid along the nav grid (goals on furniture snap to the nearest free spot; `via` points route round things the grid doesn't know, like cars), eases in, turns to `face`, then onArrive (no progress for `timeout` ticks: onFail). Returns the scene runner's waitable `{ done(), ok(), cancel() }`. The player's walk feeds her movement input (engine/update.js), so collisions and facing behave and any real input takes over; everyone else steps in `Walker.update` |
| `world/weather.js` | Weather conditions, climates, `WeatherSystem` (rain, wind, lightning, forecast) Wind: `windVec` points where the air travels, the way the rain slants (the city's prevailing wind is a northwesterly, sweeping down-right); rain, cloth, hair, clouds, curtains and leaves all move along it. Sky.ME's arrow points downwind. Rain overlay reuses its band tables and lit-drop buffer (`_RAIN_SCRATCH`), lamp RGB parsed once per lamp, lit colours cached (`_rainLitStyle`); particles' colours parsed once (`_particleStyle`), their life fade folded into globalAlpha. Lit rain (drops near a lamp, one lamp a drop) is batched: each drop goes into a `Path2D` bucket by lamp × band × closeness step (`RAIN_LIT_STEPS` 8), and each bucket is stroked once at its step's colour, alpha and width. A storm overlay is ~165 canvas calls instead of ~1,640, and looks the same. |
| `world/weather-response.js` | How weather reaches a character: `weatherAt` (wind on hair and cloth, rain, storm), lightning flinches, umbrellas |
| `world/particles-decals.js` | Leaf particles, decals. Leaves ride the full wind vector: they spawn off the upwind edge, ease to the wind's speed and flutter across it (no screen-south fall). |
| `world/lobby-life.js` | The Double Nights lobby's crowd: guests (portals, the doors, the desk queue, lounges, elevators, rolling luggage) and AI staff (gold bellhops with carts, Blood Moon concierges, a silver valet) — ambient walkers, not NPCs |

Street trees stay off the pavement in front of a row. A placement's `facing` ('S', 'N', 'E', 'W'; `CityLayout.facing`) goes to the building: BuildingV2 `facing` puts its door, sign awning, entrance lights, front wash and balconies on that side (`_frontGeo`, `_entrance`; a building placed with a facing gets a shopfront even without a door; the landmarks keep their own south fronts), and its landmark and Zib drop-off use the road on that side. **Street-front fill:** `autoFillAll` grows rows along every street a block fronts (`streetSides`, `_fillStreetFronts`: north and south rows, then west and east between them, mostly wall to wall, `fill` for how much of each front), facing the road with a one-light shopfront (`shopfront: 'pool'`); `scatter: [blocks]` keeps the old random placement (the Scrapyard). **Angled streets:** `addAngledRoad(x1, y1, x2, y2, name, lanes)` runs a street at any angle from one road's centre line to another's (a T at each end; `_angledEnd` finds the road there and the `mouth`, how far in its carriageway clears it). Buildings keep off its `corridor` (carriageway and pavements: `_intersectsSafeZone`); street-front fill stops the straight rows either side of it and lines it with a stepped row on each side (`_fillAngledFronts`: each building's nearest corner on its setback line, facing whichever of N, E, S, W looks most onto it). `angledPavements()` gives its two pavements as polygons, `cutPavements(paves)` cuts the other roads' pavements where it joins, its crossings (one at each end, `orientation: 'D'`) carry their `poly`, `axis` (stripes), `kerbs` (walk lights) and `ends` (where people step off), and the pedestrians' walks run down both its pavements and break for its mouths (`_mouthGaps`). The hub's is Lantern Cut, from Clinic Way to East Ave. **Curved streets:** `addCurvedRoad(points, name, lanes, {radius})` is the same along a polyline with its corners rounded off (`RoadPath.rounded`); everything above follows its `path` (`road.path`, a `RoadPath`: `at(s, lat)`, `frame(s)`, `project`, `band`), each end meeting its road square on along its first and last legs (`corridorPieces`: the corridor piece by piece, for the rows either side). The hub's is Halfmoon Bend, a lane each way from Skyline Ave round to East Ave. `generateSidewalkNetwork`: the pavement walks and the crossings; a crossing's two ends sit on the kerbs either side of the road it crosses (a vertical road's crossing runs left to right) and join the pavement walks near them (within 130 px, else the nearest: never a dead end) Bigger cities: `ZONE.RESERVED` keeps a block clear and plain (the graveyard's); `autoFillAll({ fillPartial: [blocks] })` builds street rows round the landmarks in those blocks; `setLandmarkLampColors({ id: colour })` gives a landmark's block its lamp colour, and a block holding two landmarks is split, each lamp taking the colour of the landmark nearest it. An angled street's stepped rows are laid before the straight rows round them, and round a bend each faces whichever way meets the street first (`_facingOnto`). |

## buildings/
**A new landmark (or new art on one)**, so it stays cheap and is never cut off at the screen's edge:
1. Declare how far past its leaned outline it draws: `topReach` (base and roof passes), `glowReach` (glow without beams), `emissiveReach` (all glow). Without them it's still drawn correctly, just whenever it's near.
2. Run `node tools/measure-landmark-reach.cjs` (Playwright). It prints the reach each pass needs against what's declared, and fails if any needs more; declare what it needs plus a margin.
3. Paint still roof art through `PlaneSprites.draw` (`entities/prop-sprites.js`), with `PropPass.seg(live)` round anything that moves.
4. Draw a bulb (a core and its glow) as one stamp with `bulbSprite` (`core/draw-helpers.js`), not a glow plus a dot.
